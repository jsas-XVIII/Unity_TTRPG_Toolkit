# Chapter IV: Core Rules

## What You Need to Play
- Standard polyhedral dice set: d4, d6, d8, d10, d12, d20, d100
- Pens/pencils, large sheet for drawing maps
- Character sheet or pre-made character
- Optional: miniatures, tokens/poker chips

---

## Resolution Mechanic

**The Core Roll:**
```
2d10 + [Relevant Attribute] + [Any Bonuses] vs. Target Number (TN)
```
- **Meet or exceed TN** → Success
- **Below TN** → Failure (see Failing Forward for nuance)

Used for **all** actions: combat attacks, skill checks, social interactions, exploration.

**In combat:** TN is usually the enemy's Defense Rating (DR) or Mental Resistance (MR).

---

## Difficulty & Target Numbers

| Difficulty | TN | Description |
|------------|-----|-------------|
| Simple | 5 | Everyday routine; very low chance of failure |
| Easy | 8 | Consistently achieved by the average person |
| Standard | 12 | Requires focus |
| Demanding | 15 | Requires both focus and skill |
| Challenging | 18 | Requires high level of focus and skill |
| Heroic | 22 | Achievable only by the extraordinary or lucky |
| Outstanding | 26 | Your exploits will be spoken of throughout the lands |
| Legendary | 30 | You might have a statue made for you |

GMs should use in-between numbers freely for nuance and tailoring.

## Benefit & Hindrance

| State | Roll |
|-------|------|
| **Benefit** | Roll 3d10, discard the lowest die |
| **Hindrance** | Roll 3d10, discard the highest die |

**Stacking rules:**
- Benefit/Hindrance do NOT stack with each other or with similar power effects
- They cancel on a 1-to-1 basis (2 Benefit + 1 Hindrance = 1 Benefit net)

**Recharge and Benefit/Hindrance:**
- When rolling with Benefit: any 2 matching dice out of 3 triggers Recharge
- When rolling with Hindrance: doubles do NOT trigger Recharge

**Common sources of Hindrance:**
- Using weapons/armor without Competency
- Dual wielding without proper Competency
- Adjacent enemy making a Ranged Basic Attack (powers exempt)
- Various status effects

## Spark Points

Spark Points reward players for vivid, cinematic descriptions of their actions. They are **communal** — shared across the party.

### Spark Die
- Represented by a single die visible on the table
- Die faces = max Spark Points (use d12 for 4 players standard)
- Die goes up by 1 each time a Spark Point is awarded

### Spark Rules (4 players, standard)
- Max capacity: 12 (1d12)
- Spending **4 Spark Points** = 1 **Moment of Glory** → Benefit on the next action

### Adjusting Spark
- More players → increase max capacity and/or Moment of Glory threshold
- Very talkative group making game too easy → lower capacity or raise threshold

## Ruin

Ruin is a **GM Resource** representing bad luck, misfortune, and the Drift's influence.

### Accumulating Ruin
- Passage of time
- Player actions (unsavory choices, reckless behavior)
- Resting (Respite: +3 Ruin; Full Rest: +7 Ruin)
- Stored and carried over between sessions

### Spending Ruin
| Effect | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **Monster Powers** | Activate monster special abilities that have a Ruin cost |
| **Breaches** | Ruin reaches critical → tear in reality opens, Fell pour through |
| **Forced Intrusion** | Guarantee a monster interrupts the party's rest |
| **Empowered Encounter** | Add extra enemies or reinforcements mid-fight |
| **Life is Difficult** | Make an NPC hostile, rust a lock, cause a "bad day" for anyone the party interacts with |

If not using Ruin mechanic: treat Ruin cost as cooldown in rounds (e.g., 2 Ruin cost = 2-round cooldown, plus any baseline recharge rounds).

## Failing Forward

Failing a roll doesn't always mean nothing happens. If a player's roll is **barely under the TN**, the GM is encouraged (but not required) to create an interesting consequence instead of a flat "you fail."

### Principles
- Use failing forward when it's easy and makes narrative sense
- Don't force it for straightforward tasks (picking a lock in an empty room)
- In combat: powers and attacks already have explicit failure conditions (half damage, etc.)
- Outside combat: the failure can spiral into something interesting (side quests, revelations, NPC complications)

### Example Failure Outcomes (for a theft attempt):
1. Character gets item but leaves evidence (sauce on clothing, noise)
2. Character drops the item; creates a character-defining moment
3. Character succeeds but is spotted by a witness (new complication)

---

## Always Round Down

Whenever a result is a fraction, **always round down to the nearest whole number**.

- Exception: If halving a value of 1 (which would give 0.5), always use **1** instead
- Some specific mechanics may explicitly state an exception; follow what that mechanic says

**Half Level (HL)** follows this rule: HL = Level ÷ 2, minimum 1.

## Resting & Recuperations

### Types of Rest

| Rest Type | Duration | HP Recovery | Resource | Recuperations | Ruin Generated | Necessity Cost |
|-----------|----------|-------------|----------|--------------|---------------|----------------|
| **Respite** | ~45 min | Roll Recuperations | Recharge die once | Use Recuperations | +3 | 1 Necessity |
| **Full Rest** | ~7 hours | Full HP restored | Full restore | Half of max Recuperations restored | +7 | 1 Necessity |

**Without Necessities:** All benefits of Respite or Full Rest are **halved**.

### Recuperations
- All Maintained Powers deactivate during a Full Rest
- **Baseline**: 2 Recuperations
- **Additional**: +1 per 2 points of MIGHT
- **Per level**: varies by class
- **Using a Recuperation**: Roll your class's Recuperation Die; restore that many HP
  - Example: Sentinel (d10) spends 3 Recuperations → rolls 3d10, sums the result = HP restored

### Intrusions (during Full Rest only)
Intrusions do NOT trigger during Respite.

When taking a Full Rest outside a truly secure location:
1. Designate one party member to roll 1d10
2. GM declares 2 numbers (1–10) before the roll
3. If result matches either number → Intrusion occurs

**Intrusion rules:**
- Party is always **Surprised** (loses First Strike / Speed check advantage, unless a power says otherwise)
- Must defeat intruders before completing the rest
- If any party member is Incapacitated during the Intrusion fight → must spend another Necessity to receive Full Rest benefits

## Environmental Dangers

### Extreme Conditions
When in harsh environments (extreme heat, extreme cold, toxic atmosphere) without proper protection:
- Periodically make 2d10 + MIGHT checks vs. the environment's TN
- Failure results in environmental damage

| Intensity | TN | Damage |
|-----------|-----|--------|
| Mild | 13 | 1d4+1 |
| Moderate | 15 | 1d6+2 |
| Heavy | 17 | 1d8+4 |
| Extreme | 20 | 1d10+6 |

**Check frequency:** Every 10–15 minutes of in-game time (GM discretion).

### Falling
- **1d8 Physical damage per 3 meters fallen**
- In combat: always treated as Staggered at the end of the fall (if enough distance to take damage)

### Suffocation & Drowning
- Hold breath: ~1 minute normally + 5 seconds per point of MIGHT
- After limit: suffer **50% of max HP as damage every 10 seconds**

## Sustenance

Characters can function for **1.5 days** without food and water.

Beyond that (each day): **−1 to all 4 Main Attributes** (stacks daily)

If penalty reaches **−4**: character is **Incapacitated** until fed and given a Full Rest.

Each Respite or Full Rest consumes 1 Necessity (which includes meal rations), so sustenance is passively tracked through Necessity usage.

---

## Optional GM Dice Rolling

Default rules: GM does **not** roll dice; all player actions are resolved by player rolls vs. set TNs. This allows the GM to focus on narration.

**Optional:** GM rolls dice too. See the GM Guide (Chapter IX) for alternate rules. Trade-off is slightly less narrative cohesion for the visceral satisfaction of rolling.
